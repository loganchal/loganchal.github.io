## Proposed content design

Retain the film-grained photographic opening. Below it, use a cream reading surface, an eight-pixel spacing rhythm, readable text measures, fine rules, a shared type scale and generous figure space. Prioritize a short research introduction, selected oceanography/mathematics work, selected papers, code/data/proof links, notes, CV and contact. Use typographic rows rather than boxed cards. Do not add empty sections just to complete a template.

A visual model breakdown should be a dedicated, directly linkable research page, for example /research/ocean-model/. Structure it around the scientific question, conceptual system, model components, forcing and experiments, diagnostics, principal results, validation and the author's specific contribution. Use actual model figures, not invented scientific diagrams. Captions should explain what each visual demonstrates; the explanation should remain readable without interaction. A small interactive layer can then reveal components or scenarios. Keep methods, assumptions and links to code/data available beside the explanation.

## PhD applicant guidance

Stanford's general advice emphasizes personal creative contributions and lessons from research experiences: https://www.cs.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj28076/files/media/file/general-advice-for-cs-phd-applicants.pdf . This is CS-specific advice, not a universal admissions rule.

Jonathan Aldrich's CMU advice asks applicants to explain the research problem, why it is difficult, their technical solution and how it was validated: https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aldrich/essay-advice.html . Our application to the site is to make those elements easy to inspect on each research page.

Oxford's general supervisor guidance emphasizes research fit: https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/graduate/application-guide/starting-your-application/research-project-and-supervisor . Its Mathematics FAQ has discipline-specific application details: https://www.maths.ox.ac.uk/study-here/postgraduate-study/frequently-asked-questions . The site should make interests and contributions clear, while application requirements remain programme-specific.

## Discoverability

Google recommends descriptive titles, accessible page text, crawlable links, and clear images beside relevant text: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide and https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images . The visual model explanation should therefore have a descriptive permanent URL, a unique title, real HTML headings and text, descriptive figure filenames, captions and appropriate alt text. The interactive graphic is an enhancement to that explanation.

The current release adds a canonical URL, basic Person/WebSite structured data, a sitemap, robots.txt and noindex tags for design-study/image-library pages. This does not guarantee rankings or indexing. Canonical scheme follows the working custom-domain transport until GitHub provisions HTTPS. Add Search Console verification when account access is available; only then request indexing and inspect actual crawl results. Do not invent CV, ORCID, Scholar or publication metadata.
